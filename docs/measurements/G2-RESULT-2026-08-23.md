## O4 — `dsp_max` 78.3 vs `HIGH` 78.0 (closed)

The negative arm reported `dsp_max=78.341919` with `governor_engagements_total=0`. That
looked like a 0.3 pp near-miss. It was not — **78.0 and 78.3 were never the same quantity.**

| quantity | source | rate |
|---|---|---|
| Governor `cpu` | `SurgeCpuMonitor.raw_percent` — `/proc` jiffies (or OSC if ever supported) over the poll window | ~6.7 Hz (`MPE_POLY_POLL_INTERVAL_S=0.15`) |
| Soak `dsp_max` | `jack_cpu_load` max over the run | ~1 Hz (background sampler) |

Code: `patch_browser/surge_poly_governor.py` `_cpu_sample()` prefers raw proc/OSC; the `high`
path needs `cpu >= MPE_POLY_CPU_HIGH` for `MPE_POLY_CPU_HIGH_HOLD_S` (0.15 s). The `spike`
path uses `MPE_POLY_CPU_SPIKE` (default 78.0, independent of HIGH).

**Two instruments, not one.** JACK `dsp_max` and proc/OSC CPU differ in denominator and
sampling rate. The negative arm demonstrated **non-engagement** at clean load — not a tight
margin at HIGH. G2's `HIGH=78.0` / `LOW=68.0` are calibrated against the governor's meter;
that is what counts. Do not tune them from soak `dsp_max`. The positive arm (Crystals @6,
proc CPU hit 100 → emergency) confirms the governor is live.
