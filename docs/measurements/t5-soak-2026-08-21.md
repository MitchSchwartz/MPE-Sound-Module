## The number that matters: this does not contradict T4c

T4c measured 1024×3 with 16 loops at **0.13/min** (58/60 clean) and that became
the shipping claim. This soak measured **0.93/min** on the same buffer and the
same loop count. That looked like a 7× regression on the longer window.

It is not. **T4c ran condition B; the soak ran condition D.** Both used
`SECONDS_PER_RUN=60`, so the windows are directly comparable — the conditions
are not. D adds the rest of the running stack on top of B.

The cost is consistent with the ladder measured at 512, where B→D was
2.27 → 3.13 (+0.86/min). Here B→D is 0.13 → 0.93 (**+0.80/min**). Same
direction, same magnitude. The stack costs roughly +0.8 xruns/min regardless of
buffer size, which is itself a useful finding: it looks like a fixed per-minute
cost, not something that scales with the period.

## What this changes

The "64 ms, up to 8 loops, clean" claim was measured **without the full stack
running**. Under condition D — what actually runs on the appliance — 1024×3
with 16 loops produces about one audible glitch per minute, sustained, for
eight hours.

Two consequences:

1. **The shipping claim needs re-qualifying.** Either state it as condition B,
   or re-measure the loop curve at 1024 under D. T4c's 0/4/8-loop cells were all
   0.00 at condition B; the 8-loop cell under D is unmeasured and is the one the
   claim rests on.
2. **The +0.8/min stack cost is now the largest single term** — larger than
   anything buffer geometry is likely to recover. T7a can still tell us whether
   the limit is cushion or deadline, but a win there does not remove this.
